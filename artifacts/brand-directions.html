Snackbyte, round three

Chosen: the row of two nibbles, granite then sky, with Bricolage setting the name. That is the logo. The icon is the same two nibbles stacked, because a row is a poor icon.

Getting from the row to the icon took four decisions, and each is shown here as the options it was, every option by day and by night. My pick in each is marked and given its reason; the other options stand on their own and the marking is only a recommendation. The last section puts my picks in use so you can see them land.

Where the row sits relative to the name. Settled: both are used. Above is the primary; beside is the low-height alternative for headers, letterhead, and anywhere height is scarce.

Above shares the name's left edge and lets the row be set a little narrower than the word; it is the poster form and the site form. Beside is the letterhead and header form; the row sits at the cap height, and the bite reads as a notch. The rule between them is height: use above whenever there is room for it.

Stacking order, and where the bite goes

The row folds into two rows of four. Which nibble goes on top decides where the bite can be.

Sky over granite My pick

Read: reverses the row's sequence, but the bite stays at the top right, exactly where the row has it, and the stack reads as a horizon.

Why: the bite is the one detail that says snack, and this is the only order that keeps it full-size and outward-facing. The sequence flip costs nothing a viewer would notice.

Granite over sky, bite below

Read: keeps the row's sequence. The bite moves to the outer bottom corner, where it tends to read as a folded page corner rather than a bite.

Granite over sky, bite into the seam

Read: keeps the sequence and the bitten cell's corner. The bite has to shrink to stay off the granite above it, and it faces inward, so it is the least visible of the three.

The seam in the stack

The row keeps its nibbles apart with a gap of seven to the cells' three. Stacked, the colour change already marks the seam, so the wide gap is a choice, not a need. Shown with sky over granite; it applies to whichever order wins.

Wide seam, seven My pick, least sure

Read: the same rule as the row, so logo and icon are one construction. Two separate things, deliberately apart.

Why: the seam is the brief, and one construction for both forms is worth more than a tighter favicon. This is the pick I am least sure of; if the 16-pixel icon matters more than the rule, take the uniform gaps.

Uniform gaps, three

Read: denser and squarer, so it fills a tile better and holds together at 16. Reads as one object with two colours rather than two objects.

The tile, for square places

App icons and favicons need a filled square. What colour it is decides whether the icon follows the theme or stays constant. Shown at 80, 32 and 16 on a neutral surface so the tile is visible. Sage was tried and dropped: sky on sage is 2:1.
