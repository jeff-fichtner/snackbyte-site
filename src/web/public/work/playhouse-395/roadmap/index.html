Getting the week to the cast

Everything here reads the weekly tab and needs nothing else from anyone. This is the near side of the map.

Before the tab

Deciding what goes in the week

There is no industry standard here. It comes down to the director and the music director on each show. What follows is a first attempt at writing down the reasoning that seems to hold — offered as guidance to argue with, not a rule.

Hardest material first

Whatever is hardest in this show gets scheduled earliest, so there is the most possible time to review it before opening. What counts as hardest is judged case by case and is the thing that outranks everything below.

Staging wants chronological order

Working scenes in show order lets performers build a character forward instead of in fragments, and makes entrances and exits far easier for everyone to track. It is the default for blocking — and it is what hard material breaks when the two collide.

A claiming order, for musicals

Choreography calls the performers it needs first, then staging, then music works with whoever is left. The reasoning: choreo is a short front-loaded cost across relatively few numbers, staging is the thing performers cannot practise alone, and music can be absorbed in gaps and small groups.

The override

In the first weeks, a large and difficult musical number can outrank that order. Small principal work fits around anything; it is the big-cast number that cannot find a slot where music asks for time that would otherwise have gone to staging.

Preproduction fixes the frame, not the contents

The length of the rehearsal period, and roughly how many days each discipline needs, get settled before rehearsals start. How that time actually gets broken up is decided closer in, a week or two at a time.

Still open

How is “hardest” actually judged for a given show? Every principle above rests on this one, and it is the least written down.
How does cast availability get collected and folded in?
What makes a published week change once it is out?
Where does the claiming order stop working as a cast gets bigger?

Cross-cutting

The show changes the weights

None of the reasoning above holds evenly. Four things about a production shift it, and any standard worth writing has to say which kind of show it is describing.

AxisWhat shifts
Musicalvs. straight play A straight play has no music or choreo lane at all. The claiming order collapses to one discipline, and chronological staging becomes the dominant principle rather than one pressure among three.
Staging-heavyvs. concert-heavy Choreo-first assumes relatively few numbers carrying heavy staging. A score-driven show inverts it — music becomes the long pole and should be claiming performers first.
Children in the castvs. adults only Children’s release times cut the usable evening roughly in half and make the overlap hour the scarcest thing in the process. An adults-only show has no such constraint and can schedule almost anything in almost any slot.
Large castvs. small cast With a small cast the claiming order barely matters — most people are needed most of the time. As the cast grows, and as performers start covering more than one role, the order becomes the whole game.
The near side needs nothing from anyone beyond the weekly tab as it is already filled in. The far side is deliberately unresolved: those questions get answered by watching how a production actually runs, not by deciding something in advance — and answering one is what moves an idea on that side from a dashed outline to something worth building.
