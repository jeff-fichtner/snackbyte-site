The Show Playbook

Everything we have built for Matilda, put in the order a show needs it, so the next show can do it again — with an AI assistant, or entirely by hand.

Jan — you said we need other people who can do this, not just me. I agree, and this page is the start of how. Matilda is the first show where we have done the whole run of it online: auditions, callbacks, the cast list, the rehearsal call board, and now the flyer and the website for ticket sales.

None of it was a one-off. Every show goes through the same stages and needs the same pieces, just with different dates, names and artwork. So the plan is to write each stage down as a short playbook with Matilda as the worked example, so the next person does not have to invent anything, only fill it in.

The idea in one line

Fill in the show’s facts once — the dates, the place, the prices, the artwork — and every flyer, web page and schedule comes from them.

  • Seven stages, from announcing auditions to putting the photos up afterwards. Five are done or under way for Matilda.
  • Every stage has a checklist a person can follow by hand, and the same steps written so an AI assistant can carry them out using Matilda as its model.
  • Some steps become small tools, like making the flyer straight from the show’s facts, so they take minutes, not evenings.
01 · A show, stage by stage

What each stage needs, and what Matilda’s looked like

Each stage below is a set of pieces that every show needs. Under each one is what we actually built for Matilda — the real website blocks, exactly as they went up. We still have every one, ready to paste again with the next show’s words. (Names are shortened on this page.)

1. Announce auditions

JulyDone

Tell people the show is happening and how to try out.

  • Sign-up QR codeA short link with the Playhouse 395 shield in the middle. The link can be pointed somewhere new later without reprinting.
  • Auditions page and a homepage lineSo someone who hears about it finds the details on playhouse395.com.
  • Audition form and contractFilled in at the audition.
  • The show’s own page, “coming soon”playhouse395.com/matilda exists from the start, so there is one address to point people to.

3. Cast announced

AugustDone

Announce the cast and tell them what happens next.

  • The show’s own page (playhouse395.com/matilda)The cast list, plus a “what happens next” panel: first rehearsal, what to bring, who to call.

4. Rehearsals

August – NovemberRunning

Turn the production team’s weekly schedule into something each cast member can read in seconds.

  • The weekly schedule sheetThe production team keeps filling in the same Google Sheet, the way they already do.
  • The call board (rehearsal.playhouse395.com)Pick your name, see when you are called and when you are done. Updated every week from the sheet.
  • Checks before it goes upEvery name is matched against the cast list, and anything unclear on the sheet comes back as a question, not a guess. The team’s private notes column is never published.

OctoberDone

One look, one set of facts, everywhere: the flyer, the homepage and the show’s page all say the same thing and lead to the same place. It is all live; it is laid out on the Matilda Publicity page.

03 · Going forward

Three ways to do the next show

Whoever picks this up next has a choice, and all three use the same playbook.

By handA checklist for each stage: what to gather, what to fill in, where to paste it. No AI needed, and no software beyond Squarespace and Canva.
With an AI assistantGive it the playbook and the new show’s facts. Matilda is the example it follows, so it builds the next show’s pieces the same way.
With small toolsThe repetitive parts — the flyer from the facts, the QR codes, the call board — become tools that do the step in one go.
