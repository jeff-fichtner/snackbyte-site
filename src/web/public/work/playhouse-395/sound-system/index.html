Where things stand

The WING console (the full-size WING-BK, with the WING-LIVE recording card) and two VocoPro Digital-Play-16 wireless sets were bought on the June 2025 B&H quote: $6,629.49 in total, paid for in part by a $5,000 Inyo County grant. The two VocoPro sets turned out to be identical: same fixed 900 MHz frequencies, nothing can be retuned, so only one can be on at a time. That left 16 usable body mics. Everything since has been about getting back to 32.

  • Frequency plans worked out. For both wireless plans below, there is a set of frequencies that works. See section 2.

Plan 1 · ~$1,307 · at the limit

  • Cheapest way to the goal. $500 less than plan 2.
  • Runs Phenyx at exactly 16, the most they recommend. Their words: “it might not be able to reach 16.”
  • The frequency plan only works with looser spacing than the usual rule (below). Expect the occasional noise or dropout when certain actors stand close together — the kind of problem that comes and goes and is hard to pin down.
  • Fallback if it misbehaves: return the PTU-7000-4B and buy 4 Samson instead, which turns it into plan 2. Phenyx charges 10–30% to take back a used item, so $32–$96.

Plan 2 · ~$1,810 · room to spare

  • Phenyx at 12, four under their limit.
  • A frequency plan exists that meets the usual spacing rule, so the problem in plan 1 is designed out rather than tested for.
  • Three brands to look after instead of two: VocoPro, Phenyx and Samson, each with its own headsets and settings.
  • The Samson kits sit at 470–494 MHz, well clear of everything else. In stock at Sweetwater, B&H and Walmart at the time of writing.
Check before ordering either plan: the VocoPro Digital-Quad-B-500's frequencies. VocoPro says each of its 4 channels has “six switchable frequencies” in the 500 MHz band, but doesn't say which. “500 MHz” covers 470–608, and the Phenyx gear is in it too. If those frequencies are below 504 MHz, both plans stand. If they're in 520–602, the VocoPro add-on lands on top of the Phenyx gear and is out; its 4 mics would come from Samson instead, which makes plan 1 about $1,740 and plan 2 about $2,240. The manual or one email to VocoPro settles it.

Why there's a limit at all

Mics on different frequencies don't hear each other. The problem is that wireless gear is slightly imperfect: two transmitters together also give off faint copies of their signals at predictable frequencies (twice one minus the other). If a copy lands on a frequency another mic is using, that mic picks up noise. Our own handhelds at 546.30 and 561.60 MHz, for example, put a copy at 531.00. The number of copies grows much faster than the number of mics — 240 of the simplest kind with 16 mics, against 132 with 12 — and every mic has to avoid all of them. That's what Phenyx's 16 describes. The usual rule is to keep every mic at least 250 kHz from every copy. At 12 Phenyx mics that works. At 16 it only works if the weaker copies, the ones that need three transmitters close together, are allowed within 100 kHz.

Frequency plans, for whoever sets up
  • Fixed, can't change: Phenyx PTU-5000 handhelds 541.90, 546.30, 561.60, 568.65 MHz.
  • Plan 1, PTU-6000 channels 1–8: 520.65, 530.55, 540.90, 551.95, 562.75, 572.55, 587.55, 595.75 MHz. PTU-7000 channels 1–4: 538.30, 555.10, 580.90, 598.00 MHz.
  • Plan 2, PTU-6000 channels 1–8: 520.15, 530.30, 541.15, 554.20, 563.25, 572.05, 586.05, 600.75 MHz.
  • Not yet in either plan: the VocoPro 500 MHz add-on and the Samson kits. Their frequencies go into the same plan before the first show, using Shure's free Wireless Workbench software, which can model other brands.
  • Set up in this order: handhelds on, then the VocoPro and Samson mics, then set the Phenyx receivers to the planned frequencies by hand (don't rely on auto-scan) and lock them.
  • Labels: Phenyx packs A1–A8 (and B1–B4 for the PTU-7000 in plan 1), matched on the receivers and the mixer.

04 · Decided

The VocoPro sets: keep one, sell one

Keep: one VocoPro Digital-Play-16

  • 16 body mics at 900 MHz, four 4-channel receivers, fixed groups B1–B4.
  • Nothing else we own or plan to buy uses 900 MHz, so it never interferes with them.
  • Its own XLR snakes run its 16 outputs into the S32.
  • Both plans add VocoPro's 500 MHz quad to it, for 20 VocoPro body mics — the same mix as VocoPro's own 20-mic package.

Sell: the other, complete

  • Estimated $650–$850 used. Budget 900 MHz gear doesn't hold value the way Shure or Sennheiser does. A return is off the table: opened, and over a year old.
  • List it as “open box, unused” if the packs were never worn.
  • Keep it whole: all 16 bodypacks, headsets, lavs, XLR snakes, mounting brackets, original box and warranty card. Not the rechargeable batteries — those stay with us.
  • Say it's a full B1–B4 set, so VocoPro owners know whether it will collide with theirs.
  • Where: Reverb, or locally to schools and churches.

Out of the cardboard boxes. 32 bodypacks, each with a headset and a lavalier whose thin cables tangle, plus 4 handhelds, batteries and chargers — kept at the theatre, and now and then carried to another venue. These are options, not a decision.

It stops the tangling, keeps each mic with its own pack, locks, and travels.
