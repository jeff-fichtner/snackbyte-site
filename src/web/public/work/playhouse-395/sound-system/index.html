01 · Background

What we already have, and the problem

The WING console (the full-size WING-BK, with the WING-LIVE recording card) and two VocoPro Digital-Play-16 wireless sets were bought on the June 2025 B&H quote: $6,629.49 in total, paid for in part by a $5,000 Inyo County grant.

The two VocoPro sets turned out to be identical in the way that matters. Each is locked to the same fixed 900 MHz frequency groups, B1–B4, and nothing can be retuned, so only one can be on at a time. That leaves 16 usable channels, not 32. The fix: keep one set, sell the other, and add 16 channels in a different band.

1 · Flight case

ProX XS-BWING W ~$409 – $450

iDJNow lists it at $409.40, Full Compass at $449.99; Sweetwater also carries it. About 37″ × 26.5″ × 12″ and 45 lb empty. No doghouse: the case is lighter and smaller, and the cables come off for transport.

Chosen over the Gator GTOURWINGNDH ($449.99) because it rolls on four 4″ casters, two locking, which suit indoor floors; the Gator's tilt-and-go wheels are the better choice for gravel and grass.

It fits the WING-BK. The black edition is the same console in a different colour: Behringer's own guide covers “WING and WING-BK” together, at the same 201 × 870 × 575 mm and 24 kg, and Thomann sells the WING-BK bundled with a case made for the original WING. ProX's rear photo shows the connections clear of the base wall. ProX's listing only says “WING”, so it is worth a quick yes from the seller when ordering.

2 · Wireless

Phenyx Pro PTU-6000-8B ×2 $597.99 each · $1,196

Eight channels per kit in the 520–602 MHz band, well clear of the VocoPro's 900 MHz, so the two systems never collide. Two kits is the most this line supports together, which is exactly 16 channels. Each kit comes with 8 PWB-7 bodypacks, 8 headset/lavalier combos, 4 BNC antennas, a power adapter and a ¼-inch audio cable.

Buy from phenyxpro.com or Phenyx Pro's official Amazon store — the one-year warranty only holds when bought from an authorized seller. Neither Sweetwater nor B&H stocks it.

The two kits cover the same range, so they have to be set apart by hand:

  1. Power on kit 1 and its 8 bodypacks, scan, and lock.
  2. With kit 1 still running, scan kit 2 so it picks frequencies around kit 1. Then lock.
  3. Label packs, receivers and mixer channels A1–A8 and B1–B8.
  4. Re-scan at every venue. Local TV signals, including the Owens Valley translators, change from place to place.
Before ordering, email Phenyx support for their recommended two-system frequency plan for the 8B.

3 · Stage box

Behringer S32 $669.99 – $689

$669.99 at Full Compass, $689 at Sweetwater. The S32 puts 32 mic inputs at the far end of a single AES50 cable. With the WING's own 8 mic inputs and 8 line inputs, that makes 40 mic and 8 line — enough for all 32 wireless channels plus 8 more mics for a small pit, announce or talkback.

03 · Decided

The VocoPro sets: keep one, sell one

Keep: one VocoPro Digital-Play-16

  • 16 channels at 900 MHz, four 4-channel receivers, fixed groups B1–B4.
  • Works alongside the Phenyx kits — different band, no shared frequencies.
  • Its own XLR snakes run its 16 outputs into the S32.
  • Assign by system, e.g. ensemble on one, leads on the other. Interference is much easier to trace during tech.

Sell: the other, complete

  • Estimated $650–$850 used. Budget 900 MHz gear does not hold value the way Shure or Sennheiser does. A return is off the table: opened, and over a year old.
  • List it as “open box, unused” if the packs were never worn.
  • Keep it whole: all 16 bodypacks, headsets, lavs, XLR snakes, mounting brackets, original box and warranty card. Not the rechargeable batteries — those stay with us.
  • Say it is a full B1–B4 set, so VocoPro owners know whether it will collide with theirs.
  • Where: Reverb, or locally to schools and churches.

05 · Options

Storing the mics

Out of the cardboard boxes. There are 32 bodypacks to keep, each with a headset and a lavalier whose thin cables tangle, plus batteries and chargers — kept at the theatre, and now and then carried to another venue. These are options, not a decision.

OptionCost
A numbered pouch for every channel One zip mesh pouch per mic: the pack, its headset, its lav and spare batteries together, labelled to match the channel (A1–B8, and the VocoPro channels). Nothing tangles and nothing gets separated. Example: vxb mesh zipper pouches with label pockets, $49.98 for 20. Suggested~$100
A hard case to carry the pouches The pouches go into a lockable, weatherproof case for storage and for trips. Example: Harbor Freight Apache 4800 X-Large, $59.99, interior 17.9″ × 12.9″ × 6.6″. Plan one or two. Suggested$60 – $120
A numbered organizer backstage For show nights: an over-the-door 24-pocket organizer, each pocket numbered. Actors drop their pack and mic in their own pocket, and it all goes back into the case at the end of the night. Optional$8 – $20
Tackle-style divider boxes Plano StowAway 3700 Deep, $14.99, adjustable dividers. About six packs with their headsets per box, so around six boxes — and the lavs would likely need more. Alternative~$90+
A locking rolling tool cart U.S. General 30″ 5-drawer cart, $289.99. Very secure, and one drawer would hold every pack. A good permanent home, but heavy to take anywhere else. Alternative$290
Purpose-built wireless cases SKB iSeries 3i-1813-7WMC, $309.99, holds only 8 bodypacks (and 8 handheld mics we don't have). Nothing off the shelf is made for 16 bodypacks. Not a fit~$1,240
The suggestion: numbered pouches inside one or two Apache cases, about $160–$220, with a backstage organizer for show nights. It solves the tangling, keeps each mic with its own pack, locks, and travels. Take the batteries out before storing, add a few silica gel packs, and measure a VocoPro pack before buying, since its size isn't published.
