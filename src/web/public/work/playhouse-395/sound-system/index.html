Total28 + 4All 32 S32 inputs
  • Every brand is under its own limit. Phenyx says no more than 16 of its mics at once; we run 12. VocoPro's set plus its add-on is exactly the 20-mic package VocoPro sells.
  • The frequency plan meets the standard rule — every mic at least 250 kHz from the faint stray signals the others create. Most of that headroom goes away once the Samson mics are added (section 4).
  • No mic sits on a TV station. Only one station reaches the school (506–512 MHz), and every mic is set clear of it.
  • The WING's own 8 mic inputs stay free for the pit, announcements and talkback.
Frequency plan, for whoever sets up
  • Phenyx handhelds (fixed, can't change): 541.90, 546.30, 561.60, 568.65 MHz.
  • VocoPro add-on: Q on 470.5 (1 blink), R on 502.8 (2 blinks), S on 482.6 (1 blink), T on 488.6 (1 blink). R must be 502.8 — its first setting, 478.3, collides with the handhelds. Never use T's sixth setting, 608.53 MHz: that frequency isn't legal for unlicensed mics in the US.
  • Phenyx PTU-6000 channels 1–8: 521.40, 537.80, 542.65, 558.70, 569.75, 571.80, 583.30, 598.25 MHz. Set these by hand; don't rely on auto-scan, which doesn't account for stray signals.
  • The 900 MHz VocoPro set stays on its own groups B1–B4 and doesn't interact with any of the above.
  • Order: handhelds on first, then the VocoPro add-on, then set the Phenyx receiver channel by channel and lock it. Label every pack, receiver channel and mixer channel to match.
  • At another venue, re-check: a different TV station can sit on these frequencies.

04 · Paused

After the show

We're holding at 28 body mics for Matilda. After it closes on November 21:

  • Decide on the last 4 body mics, to reach 32. The pick is four Samson Concert 88x headset systems in the K band, 470–494 MHz (model SWC88XBHS5K), about $191–$205 each at Sweetwater, B&H or Walmart — roughly $820. It has to be the K band: Samson's D band sits on top of the Phenyx gear.
  • Sell the duplicate VocoPro set (section 6).
  • Pick a way to store the mics (section 7).
  • Consider better headsets for the leads. The included headsets are the weak point of budget systems; better ones improve the sound more than any receiver.
  • Optional: tablet control. A small Wi-Fi router on the WING's network port lets the board be mixed from a tablet with Behringer's WING Copilot app.
What adding Samson changes. Four Samson mics keep every brand under its limit (Samson's is 8), but they crowd the low range the VocoPro add-on uses. The frequency plan then only works if the faintest stray signals — the ones that need three particular mics on and close together — are allowed within 100 kHz instead of 250. That's a smaller margin than the show setup, still well short of running any brand at its limit. Every Phenyx and VocoPro frequency gets re-set at that point:

05 · Record

How we decided

  • The starting problem. The two VocoPro sets bought in June 2025 turned out identical — same fixed 900 MHz frequencies, nothing can be retuned — so only one can run at a time. That left 16 body mics instead of 32.
  • No more 900 MHz. VocoPro's 900 MHz gear has only four frequency groups, and the set we kept uses all four. Anything more had to go in the 500 MHz range.
  • Phenyx's limit. Our 4 handhelds are Phenyx (a PTU-5000 on fixed frequencies). Phenyx told us, in writing, that 16 of its mics at once is the maximum and may not be reachable. Running at 16 would only have worked with tighter spacing than the usual rule, so we stopped at 12: the handhelds plus one 8-mic kit.
  • Why the VocoPro add-on. Its manual lists all 24 preset frequencies; each channel has a setting below 504 MHz, clear of the Phenyx gear and the TV station. It's cheap ($389), designed to run with our set, and the full plan meets the standard rule.
  • Why the last 4 wait. Samson is the only budget brand found below the Phenyx range. Adding it cuts the spacing margin, and 28 body mics plus 4 handhelds is enough to run the show. Whether to add it is a decision for after Matilda.
  • Ruled out: a second 8-mic Phenyx kit (past Phenyx's limit), 8 Samson (exactly at Samson's limit of 8), anything at 900 MHz (collides with our VocoPro set), and pro systems from Shure or Sennheiser (roughly three times the cost).
06 · Decided

The VocoPro sets: keep one, sell one

Keep: one VocoPro Digital-Play-16

  • 16 body mics at 900 MHz, four 4-channel receivers, fixed groups B1–B4.
  • Nothing else we own uses 900 MHz, so it never interferes with the rest.
  • Its own XLR snakes run its 16 outputs into the S32.
  • The new 500 MHz add-on joins it for 20 VocoPro body mics.

Sell: the other, complete

  • Estimated $650–$850 used. Budget 900 MHz gear doesn't hold value the way Shure or Sennheiser does. A return is off the table: opened, and over a year old.
  • List it as “open box, unused” if the packs were never worn.
  • Keep it whole: all 16 bodypacks, headsets, lavs, XLR snakes, mounting brackets, original box and warranty card. Not the rechargeable batteries — those stay with us.
  • Say it's a full B1–B4 set, so VocoPro owners know whether it will collide with theirs.
  • Where: Reverb, or locally to schools and churches.
