What helps and what hurts

In its favour

  • 20 channels of GB30 preamps — the same preamp Soundcraft fitted to its larger LX7ii and GB series consoles, and the main reason these hold their value.
  • Built-in Lexicon effects running the same processor as the Lexicon MX400, so there is no outboard reverb unit to buy or patch in.
  • It has spent its life in the case, which is why the faders and pot shafts are clean. Most surviving examples are visibly beaten up.
  • Nothing to go obsolete. No firmware, no drivers, no app. It will work the same way in ten years as it does now.

Against it

  • New digital mixers cost about the same. A Behringer XR18 is $509 new, an X32 Rack $699, an X32 Producer $580 — with recallable scenes and remote control this cannot do.
  • It is analog, and it is large. Twenty channels of physical strips take real desk space and every setting has to be dialled in by hand each time.
  • It is discontinued. Soundcraft no longer sells it; parts and service would mean a third-party tech.
  • Two aux sends, not three. On this variant one send is dedicated to the internal effects, which limits monitor mixes if you need several.

Condition

The console came out of the case carrying years of gaffer tape and label residue across the fader strip and the rear panel. That has been cleaned off.
