One idea: bounded pieces, and the seam between them

Everything on this page is that sentence drawn.

snackbyte makes small, whole tools for the community around one person in the Eastern Sierra: a theatre's call board, a corkboard for the 395, a console for the day. Each tool is bounded. It does one job, knows only what it needs, and hands off cleanly to the next thing.

That is a way of building software, and it is also a temperament. It shows up in the code as one place that knows a thing, and everywhere else not knowing it. The style takes the same rule and makes it visible.

The name already says it. A byte is eight bits: the smallest unit that is a whole thing. A byte is two nibbles of four: the smallest named piece of a byte, a bounded piece inside a bounded piece. And a snack is a portion that is complete on its own.

So the mark is a byte, drawn as two nibbles, with a bite taken out of it.

The cells

%brand:geometry.cell|Word% wide, corners of %brand:geometry.radius|word%, %brand:geometry.gap|word% apart. %brand:@cells|Word% of them, because a byte is eight bits. They are the same size and the same shape, and nothing else on the mark is a shape.

The seam

Between the fourth cell and the fifth the gap is %brand:geometry.seam|word%, not %brand:geometry.gap|word%. That is the seam between the two nibbles: two bounded pieces beside each other, never touching. Separation of concerns, as a distance. The color change then confirms what the gap already said.

The bite

A circle of radius %brand:geometry.bite.r|word%, centred %brand:@biteOutside|word% unit outside the last cell's top-right corner, taken away. It is the snack. It is the one thing on the mark that is not a rule, and it always sits at the end.

%brand:color.ink.name.day%, then %brand:color.sky.name.day|lower%

The two nibbles are the two colors of the valley: the Sierra, and the sky over it. Which is also, left to right, the ground and the accent of everything else on the page.

Reversing the row's order is the only fold that keeps the bite full-size, outward-facing, and at the top right where the row has it. The bite is the one detail that says snack, so it wins over sequence. Read top to bottom, it is a horizon.

The seam stays wide

%brand:geometry.seam|Word% between the rows, like %brand:geometry.seam|word% between the nibbles. One construction for both forms; the icon is the logo, not a cousin of it.

The tile

Square places need a filled square. It takes the ground color of whichever theme it sits in: %brand:color.ground.name.day% by day, %brand:color.ground.name.night% at night. That is the same rule as every other surface, so the icon never has to explain itself. Bare, the stack holds to 32; on the tile it keeps both colors to 16 and its bite to 32.

Never beside the name

The stack is for square places only. The row is for wide places, beside or above the name. Two forms of one mark, each with one job.

The palette, from the Owens Valley

%brand:@roles|Word% roles, each with a day value and a night value. Left half of a chip is day; right half is night. The colors are the valley's, named for where they come from.

%brand:@palette%

Where the names come from

%brand:color.ground.name.day% is the dry lake by day, and %brand:color.ground.name.night% the tablelands north of town at night. %brand:color.ink.name.day% is the Sierra; ground and ink trade places at night. %brand:color.sky.name.day% is deep by day and lifted at night. %brand:color.sage.name.day% is the valley floor, too light to read on %brand:color.ground.name.day%. %brand:color.bitterbrush.name.day% is the May bloom, held in reserve.

How the palette moves

Night is not a second palette. It is the same roles with the ground and the ink swapped and the accents lifted enough to read. Anything built from the roles, marks included, changes theme without being redrawn. Every text role passes 4.5:1 on its ground in both themes; %brand:color.sky.name.day% by day was darkened to %brand:color.sky.day% for exactly that.

The type, two faces

Two faces on any page. %brand:type.families.bricolage.family% draws the name and sets the page's title, and nothing smaller; %brand:type.families.inter.family% sets everything below it. A legal or financial document swaps in %brand:type.families.sourceSerif.family% below its title. Code is the system's monospace.

The voice, extracted

The words were already sounding like this in the docs before there was a style. These are the rules they were following.

Say what it does, then what it doesn't

"It reports; it does not score." "It stores no passwords." The negative clause is the signature move: the seam, in prose. Every tool gets one.

Short declaratives, semicolons doing logic

No adjectives that sell. "A console over the systems you already use" is a complete pitch with no praise in it.

Name from the concept, never the domain

Slate, Grimoire, Cue; spells, lanes; lit, banked, out. A product's name is what it means, not what it handles.

Explain before you show

The concept comes before the code; this page opens with the idea before the mark. The site does the same.

Specific over general

Bishop's community theatre. The 395. Two WEN 56360iX generators. A real noun beats a category every time.

Sentence case, plain verbs, one job per sentence

No all-caps, no "Submit," no exclamation marks. Dry humour is allowed, rare, and never signalled.

Two registers, one voice

The site explains; the tools just say. A call board tells a cast member when they're called and does not philosophise. Same rules, fewer of them.

The name is lowercase, always

snackbyte, in the wordmark and in every sentence, including the first word of one. The only exception is the legal name on legal paper.

The headline

%brand:copy.headline% The seam as a claim, with the negative clause built in. Under it: "%brand:copy.subhead%" "Building something good" is retired.

The rules, in one place

  • The row is for wide places; the stack is for square places.The stack is never set beside the name.
  • Above the name whenever there is room; beside it when there is not.Height is the only thing that decides.
  • Cells of %brand:geometry.cell|word%, gaps of %brand:geometry.gap|word%, a seam of %brand:geometry.seam|word%, a bite of radius %brand:geometry.bite.r|word%.Both forms are built from these four numbers and nothing else.
  • The bite is always at the end, always at the top right, always full size.It is the only part of the mark that is not a rule.
  • Ground and ink swap at night; the accents lift.One palette, two values per role, no redrawing.
  • %brand:color.sage.name.day% and %brand:color.bitterbrush.name.day% are for marks and surfaces, not for words.Where sage must be read, it becomes %brand:color.sage-text.name.day% by day.
  • Two faces on a page, sentence case.%brand:type.families.bricolage.family% for the name and the title, %brand:type.families.inter.family% for the text, %brand:type.families.sourceSerif.family% for legal paper.
  • The name is lowercase, always.In the wordmark and in prose, even at the start of a sentence. Legal paper is the only exception.
  • Say what it does, then what it doesn't.Every tool gets its negative clause.
